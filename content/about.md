I am a Postdoctoral Scholar - Research Associate in Professor Krishna Garikipati's [Computational Physics Group](https://sites.usc.edu/compphys/) at the University of Southern California. I received my Ph.D. in [Theoretical and Applied Mechanics](https://mechse.illinois.edu/graduate/graduate-degree-programs/phd-programs/phd-theoretical-and-applied-mechanics) with a [Data Science & Engineering graduate concentration](https://cse.illinois.edu/cse-educational-programs/dse-graduate-concentration/) from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, advised by [Professor Harley Johnson](https://mechse.illinois.edu/people/profile/htj).

My research connects the mechanisms that drive damage at small scales to the failure of real parts. I use atomistic and continuum simulation to understand how competing mechanisms decide whether a crack grows, develop machine learning solvers and test what they have learned, and combine models with sparse measurements to infer damage in parts. I contribute to the [MIRAGE project](https://mirage.cint.science/), a national-laboratory collaboration that treats materials behavior as a competition among mechanisms.

Before my Ph.D., I earned my Bachelor's and Master's degrees in mechanical engineering from the University of Wisconsin - Madison. I also worked for a decade as a mechanical design engineer on complex products ranging from healthcare devices to neutron generators (licensed Professional Engineer in the State of Wisconsin).
