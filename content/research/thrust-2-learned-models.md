Machine learning solvers can forecast damage far faster than the simulations they approximate. I am interested in what these models actually learn, and in testing them on problems where the answer is already known.

**Machine learning solvers for fracture:** I develop vision transformer solvers for stochastic phase-field fracture and a global-local approach that integrates a machine learning solver with a traditional PDE solver.

**Latent representations:** Within MIRAGE, I contribute to work on training the latent representations of neural surrogate solvers so they stay stable over long rollouts.

**Language models for mechanics:** I fine-tune large language models for computational mechanics, and contributed to AI University, an LLM-powered learning assistant for finite element methods.

**Emerging computing:** I am also exploring quantum computing for stochastic PDEs, starting with resource analysis for transport–reaction equations.

Related work: [Beyond Compression: Training Latent Representations for Stable Long-Horizon Rollout in Neural Surrogate Solvers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30198), [AI University](https://openreview.net/forum?id=NU36BROGDV) (TMLR), and [Resource Analysis for Quantum Simulation of Spatially Varying Transport-Reaction Equations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15827).
