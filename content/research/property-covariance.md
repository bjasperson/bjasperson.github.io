First-principles methods are grounded in quantum theory, but their computational cost limits the scale of simulations they can handle. 
My research addresses this by building frameworks to connect atomistic data to macroscopic behavior.

**Property Covariance:** By using ensembles of classical interatomic potentials as synthetic materials, I build regression models to uncover correlations between small-scale canonical properties and larger-scale quantities of interest, such as plastic flow strength and grain boundary energy trends. This includes generating uncertainty estimates to reliably link atomistic simulation data to macroscopic materials behavior.

**Probabilistic Frameworks:** As part of the [MIRAGE project](https://mirage.cint.science/), I contribute to probabilistic frameworks that model emergent materials behavior as mechanism ensembles.


Related publications: [Cross-scale covariance for material property prediction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-024-01453-w), [Fundamental microscopic properties as predictors of grain boundary energy trends](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actamat.2025.120722), and [Materials Behavior as Mechanism Ensembles: A Probabilistic Framework for Emergent Behaviors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27163).
