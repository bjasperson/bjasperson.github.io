First-principles methods are grounded in quantum theory, but their computational cost limits the scale of simulations they can handle. We use ensembles of classical interatomic potentials as synthetic materials to uncover correlations between small-scale canonical properties and larger-scale quantities of interest.

This work builds regression models that predict plastic flow strength and grain boundary energy trends from accessible microscopic properties, with uncertainty estimates that help connect atomistic simulation data to larger-scale materials behavior.

Related publications: [Cross-scale covariance for material property prediction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-024-01453-w) and [Fundamental microscopic properties as predictors of grain boundary energy trends](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actamat.2025.120722).
